Combos and recipes are both built from components, but work differently. A combo has no stock of its own: each sale takes stock from its components. A recipe belongs to a product with its own stock that is made ahead of time: producing takes stock from the inputs and adds the finished product, which is sold from its stock.

## Combo or recipe

- Combo: a kit, a breakfast or a dish assembled at the time of sale. Its **Stock mode** is **By components**. **Available now** shows how many can be made, and **Limited by** names the component that runs out first.
- Recipe: bread, candles or jam made in batches. The product is **Tracked**, and the recipe says what goes into one unit.

A **Not tracked** component, such as a service, adds its cost without taking any stock.

## Create a combo

1. In **Business**, **Combos and recipes**, tap **New combo**. **New product** also works, with **By components** in **Stock mode**, under **More details**.
2. Enter the name and price, and tap **Save product**. Its recipe opens.
3. **Add component** for each product, with its quantity.
4. Tap **Save combo**.

## Create a recipe to produce

1. Create the finished product as **Tracked**, the usual mode.
2. In the product details, ⋮ → **Add recipe**. The same option opens the saved recipe to change it.
3. **Add component** for each input, with what goes into one unit.
4. Tap **Save recipe**.

The **Combos and recipes** list shows only combos: a tracked product's recipe opens from its details.

## Produce

1. Tap **Produce** in the product details or in its recipe. In **Inventory** and in **Suggested reorder**, a product with a recipe says **Produce** instead of **Restock**. **New production**, in **Business**, **Production**, also works.
2. Enter the **Quantity**. The **Inputs** come from the recipe and can be changed for that batch.
3. Tap **Record production**.

The inputs go down and the finished product goes up. If an input is short, its row shows how much would be left below zero, but production can still be recorded. A wrong production is opened in **Production** and voided with **Void production**: the inputs come back and the finished product goes out.

Production can also be said with **Say**: "made 20 empanadas", or with what went in, "made a cake with 200 grams of flour and 3 eggs". If the product has no recipe, what was said becomes its recipe, per unit, in the same record. If it has one, what was said is used only in that batch, unless **Update the recipe** is on.

## The cost

The cost of a combo is the **Component cost**: what its components cost today.

A product with a recipe has its own **Average cost**, like any tracked product. Each batch comes in at what its inputs cost that day and is averaged with the stock already there. That is why the recipe cost may not match the **Average cost**: the inputs changed cost, a batch used other quantities, or the product was also purchased. Before recording, the batch's **Total cost** and the new average cost are shown.
