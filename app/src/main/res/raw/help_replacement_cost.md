There are two costs, and each one answers a different question.

## Average cost

It is the cost of the stock, averaging the purchases. If there were 10 units that cost 6 each and 10 more are bought at 8, the average cost becomes 7. Buying at a different price changes it. Sales profit is always measured with the average cost at the time of the sale.

## Replacement cost

It is what it would cost to buy one unit again today. The last purchase updates it, and it can also be corrected by hand in the product. It helps to decide the price: if restocking costs more, selling at the old price leaves less than it seems.

In the product details, **Profit at replacement cost** shows the profit per unit if it had to be bought again. **Numbers** has the same calculation for the period. Sales profit still uses the average cost.

## What does not change

Correcting a cost does not change past sales. A return or a void recalculates the average cost from the history, without editing anything.
