A purchase on credit is merchandise received today and paid to the supplier later. What is still owed goes to that supplier's account, in **Accounts payable**.

## Buy on credit

1. In the new purchase, choose the **Supplier**: a purchase on credit needs one.
2. In **Payment**, choose **On credit**. The **Due date** is suggested from the supplier's **Payment terms**, if set, and can be changed, but not to before the purchase date.
3. If part was paid on delivery, enter it in **Paid now**. The rest stays payable.
4. Tap **Record purchase**.

Buying on credit does not change profit: the cost of the merchandise counts when it is sold.

## The list

In **Business**, **Accounts payable** shows the total **Payable** at the top and what is **Due in 7 days**. Below, one supplier per row, overdue first, with the **Overdue** or **Credit** tag. The filters are **All**, **Overdue**, **Upcoming** and **Credit balance**.

{app} sends no alerts for these due dates: check them in this list. With money accounts, **Projected cash flow**, in **Numbers**, subtracts what is due to be paid.

## Record a payment

In the supplier's account, **Record payment** asks for the **Amount**, the **Money account** if accounts are used, the **Date**, which can be past but not future, and an optional **Note**. Before saving, it shows how the balance will stand.

- A payment covers the oldest debt first.
- Paying more than owed leaves a credit balance with the supplier.
- An archived supplier with a debt can still be paid.

In the details of a purchase on credit, **Paid** and **Balance** follow the payments, and **Record payment** leads to the supplier's account.

## The supplier's account

Each row shows the balance it left: **Purchase on credit**, **Payment**, **Credit from return**, **Adjustment** or **Void**. Tapping a purchase opens it, and **Open supplier** leads to the supplier's page.

- Tap a wrong payment and void it with **Void payment**: the balance goes back to what it was and the payment stays marked, not deleted.
- A return with **Credit with supplier** lowers what is owed.
- Voiding a purchase on credit offsets its debt. If it already had payments, the balance can end up in credit.
